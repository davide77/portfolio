MINDSET SHIFTS BEFORE APPLYING
Always believe in yourself and show off your best qualities
Mindset shift #1: Selling myself is not optional, it’s essential
Your CV, website and portfolio are the most ACCEPTED places to self-promote. So
before you cringe at selling yourself, it’s important to remember that the people you are
applying to work with don’t yet know just how great you are. Which is why you’ve got to do
everything you can to communicate your value with pride, so they pick up the phone and
contact you. Selling yourself is not optional, it is essential!
Mindset shift #2: I am valued
If throughout any of this process you struggle with answering questions or articulating
yourself, we’d recommend going to get feedback from other people to remind you how
brilliant you are. Sometimes our confidence can affect how we see ourselves and we
need help from others to communicate our strengths. So if you’re struggling, have a
conversation with someone who can give you honest feedback and support to complete
a killer CV.
Mindset shift #3: Take no as a not right now
Getting ghosted in business is as common as it is in dating. So please, whatever you do,
don’t give up at your first no-reply or assume that you are the problem. Something I’ve
learned is that your career journey is often not a linear path to success so if it doesn’t
work out right now, it doesn’t mean it won’t in the future.
When you stop thinking with the emotional mindset (assuming you’re the problem) and
take more of rational outlook (considering the external factors) you’ll give yourself less
of a hard time if things don’t immediately fall into place.
So now we’ve covered your mindset shifts, let’s get started on the process.

WRITING YOUR PERSONAL BIO
The opening on your paragraph needs to sell the dream of you!
Your personal profile acts as your elevator pitch and is often the first thing people
will use to form an opinion of you on. A good place to start is by writing your bio as
if they were asking you: ‘Why should we hire you over someone else?’
This is your time to sell all your best qualities, share your work experience and your
relevant life experiences. Don’t be afraid to name drop the best businesses you’ve
worked for, awards you’ve won or professional achievements you’re most proud of.
This is your 30 seconds to shine, so don’t waste it!
QUESTIONS TO REFLECT ON
Dig deep so you can see all the great things you have to offer
1. How long have you done your job?
2. How would you describe your role?
3. What motivates you to do your job?
4. How would you describe your approach to work?
5. What industries have you worked in?
6. What types of businesses have you worked for?
7. What topics have you worked across?
8. What is your proudest career achievement?
9. Have you received industry recognition for your work?
10. How would other people describe you?
11. What influences your approach?
12. How do you think you add value to a company?
13. When did you first become obsessed with your industry?
14. What made you try the job in the first place?
15. What do you do outside of work that shows your interest in the industry?

BRING YOUR EXPERIENCE TO LIFE
Don’t leave out any of the good stuff or be too humble!
This is the place where you need to highlight all the useful information that
demonstrates what a great employee you are and how you add value to a company.
It’s important that you explain your responsibilities, what the outcomes were and
what the business, customer or client gained from your hard work. Treat every
point you include as an achievement so they see the benefits you bring. Remember
this is not the time to be modest, so spend the time to really reflect on each of
your ‘work wins’ and tell your story with pride.
QUESTIONS TO REFLECT ON
Use the questions to remind you of your contributions
1. What were your responsibilities?
2. What were the different pieces of content or work you created, delivered or
oversaw?
3. Who were you targeting and why? (Age, location, markets)
4. Were the timings significant to the achievement? Did you deliver in a short time
frame or work for a long period of time?
5. Did you help to streamline a process?
6. Did this achievement mean the company you worked for could offer new
services?
7. Did you show good leadership skills or teamworking abilities, if so how?
8. Did you receive impressive feedback worth sharing?
9. Did you work alone or with others?
10. Did you help to win new work or clients for the company?
11. Has your work been recognised externally (press coverage or awards)?
12. Were there any times where you had to work under pressure?

SHARE YOUR HOBBIES AND INTERESTS
But only if they are going to help sell your skills
Your hobbies and interests are an opportunity for you to share the things you
do outside of work that an employer might be interested in. For every example
you give, consider how you can make it relevant to the industry, job or company
you want to work for. If you have the exact same work experience as another
candidate, impressive hobbies or interests could be the thing that helps you!
QUESTIONS TO REFLECT ON
Use the questions to remind you of your contributions
1. What skills do you need to carry it out?
2. Why are you passionate about this?
3. How does having this hobby develop your skills in the workplace or your career?
4. Could you use and apply your learnings from this hobby in this specific job?
5. Does this interest help your reputation in your chosen industry?
6. Could it add value to your company’s reputation?
7. Have you built a good network as a result?
8. Is it appealing to the customers or clients you work for?
9. If it was voluntary who are the people you helped?
10. If it is solution based, can you explain the problem you are helping to solve and
why?
TOP TIP: Share the things that offer an insight into what your values and
motivations are, get them excited about you! And if you don’t currently
have any hobbies or interests think of something you could pick up in
your spare time - it’s better to start now than never at all!

DO: Show your personality! It’s the one thing that sets you apart

in a long paragraph, they might miss the good stuff so keep
your 1-2 sentences long.
DO: Include all your contact details at the top of your CV so
they are easy to find. Always make your number, email address
and location quick to read and only share social media handles
if they will impress employers.

DON’T: Prioritise your design over content. Don’t risk
under-selling yourself because you’re trying to have a visually
pleasing layout. Keep your design simple!
DON’T: Use infographics to explain your hobbies or skills. If it’s
relevant to the industry or job you’re applying for, explain why.
Do not leave it in the hands of a bad graphic. Explain with words
the benefits of doing it.
DON’T: Include a photo of yourself (unless they have asked for
it as part of your application). Don’t let them make assumptions
about you before they’ve even had the chance to meet you.
DON’T: Use more than 1-2 fonts or multiple clashing colours.
Don’t make your CV too distracting, you want them to rate you
on your content not your design. Keep it fresh and readable so
they focus on your best qualities.
DON’T: Forget to look at the company’s website, their recent
news, projects and announcements. This will help you search
for key words, or get a better understanding of what they value
or are striving towards.

FINAL CHECKLIST
Make sure you do these final steps...
1. Show your personality! It’s the one thing that sets you apart from anyone else in
the world.
2. Have you included your number, email address and website?
3. Have you checked your spelling and grammar?
4. If you’re including social media handles, are they all up to date and reflective of
the information on your CV?
5. Have you gone through the job description and company website and made a list
of phrases or key words they use that you could include on your CV?
6. Can you find the contact name of the person you should be addressing your CV
or cover note to?
7. Have you registered to the company’s newsletter and are you following them on
social media channels?
8. Have you added the HR or recruitment team on Linkedin? If there isn’t one, try
adding the Managing Director, CEO, or Creative Director.
9. Have you got 2-3 testimonials or people who can give a nice reference? If not,
prep them in advance and ask them to be ready with a glowing review.
10. Our final tip, share a recent success on Linkedin so that if they research you
they will see something positive that reflects you.
DONE: You’re officially ready to hit send on your application. Remember to
follow up around 1 week later to check they’ve received your email and ask
when they will be starting the interview process so you have a timeline.
